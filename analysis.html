Day-three guidance: contrarian, not stupid

One rule decides each row: back the board favourite unless a sourced factor gives the underdog a real path and the price still leaves room. A lean is a near coin-flip that costs little; a swing is a 20-40% underdog that can move you up the leaderboard but loses more often than it wins. Strategy

Contrarian but not stupid: when an underdog is worth taking

In a pick'em the favourite is usually the crowd's pick, so a correct underdog is what separates a leaderboard entry from the pack. The trade-off only pays when the underdog is backed by a verifiable reason and the price still leaves room. The rules below are project strategy, not verified outcomes.

Contrarian screening rules used on this project; strategy, not verified outcomes.
#RuleHow it is verified
1Take the underdog when a roster change is recent and structural, not cosmetic.

A new in-game leader, a new star and a new coach inside one split means an unproven system, new communication and a short practice window. Aurora's 2026 rebuild is the day-one example.

Official organisation announcements, the transfer timeline and the team's own statements, then confirm the current five on a fixture-specific source.
2Take the underdog when the favourite is demonstrably fatigued and the market has not repriced.

Deep runs plus travel compress preparation. A favourite whose price already fell has no contrarian value left.

Compare the favourite's recent tournament load and travel from results pages, then check whether the offered price still reflects the earlier reputation.
3Prefer underdogs whose chance is roughly one in five or better, and expect to lose most of them.

A 20-40% pick that wins is worth the same single point as a favourite but moves you past everyone who copied the crowd. Below that band the pick is a lottery ticket, not an edge.

Use the sourced market probability on the picks page. Where no market exists, use the labelled research tier and treat it as low confidence.
4Never size up a contrarian pick on an identity or a number you cannot source.

Renamed teams, academy squads and unclear aliases are exactly where a whole pick'em slips away on a wrong team.

The identity register in this project requires an exact opponent and UTC start before any alias is used.
5Limit how many contrarian swings you take per slate.

Stacking four underdogs multiplies variance instead of climbing the leaderboard.

Count the contrarian picks shown in the screen below; treat anything beyond one or two as exposure, not insight.
6Weight the swing by what the fixture is worth, and count the streak you would break.

Hotspawn awards more points for higher tiers and a bonus for consecutive correct picks, so a contrarian miss costs the fixture's points plus the streak. In the current feed a tier-S match is worth 7 points, tier-B 3 and tier-C 2: a wrong tier-C swing costs 2 points, a wrong tier-S swing costs 7 and the bonus run with it.

Hotspawn's official how-it-works rules page, which the day-three points above link directly, plus the offered_points and points_breakdown fields in the fixture feed; expected points are then offered_points x the published probability, which is arithmetic on a sourced line, never a new probability.
Explicitly unverified.
  • Hotspawn's scoring rules are now collected from the official rules page and from the fixture feed's offered_points field: points per correct pick, more points for higher tiers (7 / 3 / 2 for tiers S / B / C in the current feed), a bonus for consecutive correct picks, picks locking before start and a weekly leaderboard reset. What is still not verifiable from any source this project can read is the exact streak-bonus formula, so no number is claimed for it and no claim is made about how many extra points a correct underdog earns relative to a favourite beyond the tier weighting.
  • No calibrated historical model exists here: contrarian picks are market-implied plus sourced context, and are not a promise of profit or of a higher leaderboard position.
